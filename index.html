Plain-English glossary
IPO
A company selling shares to the public for the first time.
Fresh issue
New shares; the money goes into the company.
OFS (offer for sale)
Existing owners sell their shares; the company gets nothing.
QIB
Large institutions (mutual funds, insurers). Their strong demand is a good sign.
Anchor investors
Big investors who commit before the IPO opens.
P/E
Share price divided by yearly profit per share. Lower means cheaper. Your limit is 25x.
D/E
Debt divided by owners’ money. Above 1 means more borrowed than owned.
Promoter pledge
Founders have borrowed against their own shares. Should be nil.
RHP
The final offer document filed with SEBI. Source for the checks marked “not verified”.

How to read

Eight filters, scored per IPO

✓ pass · ✕ fail · ? not verified (counted as no pass).

  • F1 Fresh issue at least 90% of the issue
  • F2 Proceeds used productively (capex, growth), not debt or OFS exit
  • F3 Post-issue P/E positive and at or below 25x
  • F4 Overall subscription 3x or more, with real QIB demand
  • F5 Debt/equity 1 or less, operating cash flow positive
  • F6 Revenue and PAT CAGR 15% or more
  • F7 Promoter holding 50% or more after issue, pledge nil
  • F8 Anchor investors present, unmodified auditor opinion

Lighter ✓/✕ with a year (e.g. FY25) means the value comes from an older SEBI draft offer document (DRHP), not the final RHP. “total” on F4 means overall subscription only; the QIB split was not available. Hover a cell or open a row for the source. “?” rows give the reason in the detail panel.
