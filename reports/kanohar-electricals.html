1. Company Overview

Incorporated in 1972, Kanohar Electricals Limited is engaged in the manufacturing of transformers in India. The Company caters to industries such as power transmission, railways, renewable energy and power distribution. The Company operates through two business segments: (i) transformer manufacturing and (ii) engineering, procurement and construction (EPC).

  • Recent events: IPO of ₹1,056 Cr (fresh issue 28%) at ₹632 per share, subscribed 63.6x, listed on Sep 16, 2026.
  • Latest annual results (FY26): revenue ₹663 Cr (+45% YoY) and net profit ₹130 Cr (+99% YoY).
  • Ownership: promoter holding moved from 95.47% before the IPO to 74.65% after it.

3. Qualitative Analysis

a) Business Model

Core Products/Services

  • Power, distribution and railway transformers
  • One of four RDSO-certified manufacturers of 100 MVA 132 kV Scott transformers
  • EPC segment alongside manufacturing

Revenue Streams

Revenue comes from transformer sales to utilities, railways, renewable developers and industrial customers, plus EPC. Manufacturing capacity is 19,200 MVA across two Meerut plants.

Competitive Advantages

  • RDSO certification for high-rating railway Scott transformers
  • 19,200 MVA capacity across two facilities in Meerut
  • Regional offices in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bangalore and Chennai and 526+ employees

b) Management Quality

Experience and track record: Incorporated in 1972, a long-established promoter-led transformer maker that has scaled profits rapidly in recent years. Details of the board and auditors should be verified in the RHP.

Corporate governance practices: The company is promoter-led. Our offer-document review found no auditor qualification (to be confirmed in the RHP). Board independence, committee structure and related-party transactions should be read in the RHP before investing.

c) Growth Strategy

Expansion Plans

  • Increase capacity and ratings for transmission, railway and renewable transformers
  • Expand EPC capability
  • Use of IPO proceeds: Funding the capital expenditure requirements of Company (₹64 Cr); Funding the incremental working capital requirements of Company (₹155 Cr); General Corporate Purposes (₹56 Cr).

Research and Development Initiatives

No material standalone R&D programme is highlighted in the sources reviewed; competitiveness rests on manufacturing, sourcing and distribution execution.
