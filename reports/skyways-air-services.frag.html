1. Company Overview

Incorporated in December 1984, Skyways Air Services Limited (SASL) is a well-established logistics and freight forwarding company with over four decades of experience in India’s air freight forwarding and logistics sector. The Company provides a comprehensive range of logistics solutions, including air and ocean freight forwarding, trucking, warehousing, customs broking, technology-driven express cargo and parcel delivery, and other value-added services. It has evolved from a Custom House Agent into a multi-modal logistics service provider offering end-to-end supply chain solutions across domestic and international markets.

  • Recent events: IPO of ₹583 Cr (fresh issue 68%) at ₹138 per share, subscribed 50.0x, listed on Sep 1, 2026.
  • Latest annual results (FY26): revenue ₹2,813 Cr (+25% YoY) and net profit ₹64 Cr (+33% YoY).
  • Ownership: promoter holding moved from 67.00% before the IPO to 49.25% after it.

3. Qualitative Analysis

  • Technology-driven express cargo and parcel delivery
  • Ranked No. 1 air freight forwarder by AWBs (World ACD) for 2022 to 2025

Competitive Advantages

  • Over four decades of operation
  • Airline alliances with Saudi Cargo, Air India Cargo, Emirates and Lufthansa
  • Multi-modal service offering

b) Management Quality

Experience and track record: Promoter-led; promoter holding falls from 67% to 49.25% after the issue. Over half of the objects repay borrowings and meet expenses.

Corporate governance practices: The company is promoter-led. Board independence, committee structure and related-party transactions should be read in the RHP before investing.

c) Growth Strategy

Expansion Plans

  • Use Rs 130 crore of working capital to scale volumes

  • Use of IPO proceeds: Repayment/pre-payment, in full or in part, of certain outstanding borr (₹217 Cr); Funding incremental working capital requirements of the Company. (₹130 Cr); General Corporate Purposes (₹14 Cr).

Research and Development Initiatives

No material standalone R&D programme is highlighted in the sources reviewed; competitiveness rests on manufacturing, sourcing and distribution execution.

The growth assumption (8%) is set at 60% of the average of the latest year-on-year earnings growth and the period profit CAGR, bounded between 8% and 35%, to allow for a normalising base. Quality factor is 1.15 when ROCE is at least 20% with growth of 20% or more, 0.85 when ROCE is below 12% or operating cash flow is negative, otherwise 1.0.
