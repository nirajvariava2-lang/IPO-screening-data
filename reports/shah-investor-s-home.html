1. Company Overview

Incorporated in October 1994, Shah Investor’s Home Limited is a retail broking company providing a range of financial services, with over three decades of experience in the securities market. The Company primarily offers equity and derivatives brokerage services, facilitating the buying and selling of equities, IPOs and other securities for retail customers, including resident and non-resident Indians. In addition to its core brokerage operations, the Company provides mutual fund distribution, margin funding, and stock lending and borrowing (SLB) services under the brand name “Shah Investors”.

  • Recent events: IPO of ₹90 Cr (fresh issue 100%) at ₹167 per share, subscribed 38.1x, listed on Oct 6, 2026.
  • Latest annual results (FY26): revenue ₹71 Cr (-24% YoY) and net profit ₹13 Cr (-43% YoY).
  • Ownership: promoter holding moved from 84.34% before the IPO to 62.81% after it.

3. Qualitative Analysis

a) Business Model

Core Products/Services

  • Equity and derivatives brokerage for retail clients, including NRIs
  • Mutual fund distribution and IPO services
  • Margin funding and stock lending and borrowing (SLB)

Revenue Streams

Revenue comes from brokerage, interest on margin funding, depository and distribution fees. Earnings rise and fall with equity market activity.

Competitive Advantages

  • Three decades of experience, trading member of NSE since 1995
  • Over 1,00,000 demat accounts served and more than 38,000 active clients
  • Network of 11 branches and over 181 authorised persons in Gujarat and Maharashtra

b) Management Quality

Experience and track record: Incorporated in 1994 as a promoter-led regional broker. Returns on equity have been modest (about 11% over three years) and debtor days have risen to 137, which shows margin funding exposure.

Corporate governance practices: The company is promoter-led. Our offer-document review found no auditor qualification (to be confirmed in the RHP). No promoter share pledge was disclosed in the offer document. Board independence, committee structure and related-party transactions should be read in the RHP before investing.

c) Growth Strategy

Expansion Plans

  • Expand authorised person network and branches
  • Grow mutual fund distribution and margin funding
  • Use of IPO proceeds: Funding working capital requirements of the Company (₹60 Cr); General Corporate Purposes (₹19 Cr).

Research and Development Initiatives

No material standalone R&D programme is highlighted in the sources reviewed; competitiveness rests on manufacturing, sourcing and distribution execution.

The growth assumption (8%) is set at 60% of the average of the latest year-on-year earnings growth and the period profit CAGR, bounded between 8% and 35%, to allow for a normalising base. Quality factor is 1.15 when ROCE is at least 20% with growth of 20% or more, 0.85 when ROCE is below 12% or operating cash flow is negative, otherwise 1.0.
