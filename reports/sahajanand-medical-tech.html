1. Company Overview

Incorporated in October 2001, Sahajanand Medical Technologies Limited is a medical technology company engaged in the development, manufacturing and marketing of technologically advanced medical devices, primarily focused on vascular intervention and structural heart intervention. Its product portfolio includes coronary stents, coronary balloons, transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) devices, occluders, renal stents and peripheral drug-coated balloons (DCBs). The Company focuses on research and development, precision manufacturing and clinically supported product innovation.

  • Recent events: IPO of ₹475 Cr (fresh issue 0%) at ₹344 per share, subscribed 14.2x, listed on Oct 23, 2026.
  • Latest annual results (FY26): revenue ₹1,210 Cr (+18% YoY) and net profit ₹69 Cr (+176% YoY).
  • Ownership: promoter holding moved from 40.82% before the IPO to 36.20% after it.

3. Qualitative Analysis

a) Business Model

Core Products/Services

  • Vascular intervention: coronary stents (Supraflex Cruz), balloons, renal stents
  • Structural heart: TAVI devices and occluders
  • Peripheral: drug-coated balloons

Revenue Streams

Revenue comes from sale of devices through direct, hybrid and distributor models in 77 countries. Revenue from operations was Rs 1,210.5 cr in FY26 versus Rs 1,024.9 cr in FY25 and Rs 901.6 cr in FY24 (about 15.9% CAGR).

Competitive Advantages

  • Three plants (two in India, one in Thailand) and two R&D centres
  • 119 patents granted, 78 applications pending
  • Presence across India and Europe

b) Management Quality

Experience and track record: Incorporated 2001, backed by institutional investors; promoter holding about 36% post-issue because the issue is a pure offer for sale.

Corporate governance practices: The company is promoter-led. Our offer-document review found no auditor qualification (to be confirmed in the RHP). Board independence, committee structure and related-party transactions should be read in the RHP before investing.

c) Growth Strategy

Expansion Plans

  • Expand TAVI and structural heart range
  • Grow drug-coated balloons
  • Broaden international distribution

Research and Development Initiatives

No material standalone R&D programme is highlighted in the sources reviewed; competitiveness rests on manufacturing, sourcing and distribution execution.

The growth assumption (35%) is set at 60% of the average of the latest year-on-year earnings growth and the period profit CAGR, bounded between 8% and 35%, to allow for a normalising base. Quality factor is 1.15 when ROCE is at least 20% with growth of 20% or more, 0.85 when ROCE is below 12% or operating cash flow is negative, otherwise 1.0.
