1. Company Overview

Incorporated in 2014, Moneyview Ltd. is a fintech company incorporated in India that provides digital financial services through its mobile platform. The company focuses on offering accessible and technology-driven financial solutions to individuals.

  • Latest annual results (FY26): revenue ₹3,351 Cr (+43% YoY) and net profit ₹243 Cr (+1% YoY).
  • Ownership: promoter holding moved from 23.96% before the IPO to 19.31% after it.

3. Qualitative Analysis

a) Business Model

Core Products/Services

  • Personal loans through a digital platform (flagship product)
  • Credit tracking and financial management services
  • Marketplace for insurance, credit cards, digital gold, payments and earned wage access

Revenue Streams

Revenue comes from loan origination and interest on its own book, plus fees and commissions from banks, NBFCs and insurers on its platform. Credit costs and funding costs drive profitability.

Competitive Advantages

  • Large user base: 140.28 million registered users and 48 financial partners (June 2026)
  • Technology and AI-led, unassisted user journeys that lower acquisition and servicing costs
  • Two-sided network linking users and lenders

b) Management Quality

Experience and track record: Incorporated in 2014, the founder-led fintech has scaled quickly with a profit CAGR of nearly 60% over five years. Promoter holding is low (about 19%), so governance depends on the board and institutional investors.

Corporate governance practices: The company is promoter-led. Our offer-document review found no auditor qualification (to be confirmed in the RHP). Board independence, committee structure and related-party transactions should be read in the RHP before investing.

c) Growth Strategy

Expansion Plans

  • Cross-sell insurance, cards, payments and wage access to its user base
  • Deepen lender partnerships and credit underwriting with data analytics
  • Use of IPO proceeds: Investment to drive growth in loan disbursals under default loss guara (₹325 Cr); Investment in WFPL, the Material Subsidiary, for the purpose of augmen (₹250 Cr); General corporate purposes (₹112 Cr).

Research and Development Initiatives

No material standalone R&D programme is highlighted in the sources reviewed; competitiveness rests on manufacturing, sourcing and distribution execution.

5. Investment Thesis

Key Drivers for Future Growth

  1. Credit demand from middle-income Indians underserved by banks
  2. Cross-selling across a large user base
  3. Operating leverage from digital distribution

Potential Catalysts for Stock Price Movement

  1. Quarterly disbursement and asset quality data
  2. New lender partnerships and product launches
  3. Regulatory clarity on digital lending

Positioning for Industry Trends and Challenges

  1. Digital lending is growing but faces tightening RBI rules
  2. Asset quality in unsecured personal loans is the key risk
  3. Funding costs and capital adequacy affect growth

The growth assumption (23%) is set at 60% of the average of the latest year-on-year earnings growth and the period profit CAGR, bounded between 8% and 35%, to allow for a normalising base. Quality factor is 1.15 when ROCE is at least 20% with growth of 20% or more, 0.85 when ROCE is below 12% or operating cash flow is negative, otherwise 1.0. These are the author's estimates, not company guidance. The model gap exceeded 35%, so the target is capped at 35% above or below the current price. The valuation gap points to a Buy, but the rating is held at Hold because the quality screen failed (returns below 15%, negative operating cash flow, debt-to-equity above 1.5 or debtor days above 150).
