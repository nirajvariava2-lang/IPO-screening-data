1. Company Overview

Incorporated in 1990, Kusumgar Ltd is a manufacturer of woven, coated, and laminated synthetic fabrics known as engineered fabrics. Its products are based mainly on polyamide and polyester filaments, using polyurethane chemistry. As of March 31, 2026, it has developed over 1,000 unique fabric types (SKUs), building a strong position in the synthetic functional and performance fabric space.

  • Recent events: IPO of ₹650 Cr (fresh issue 0%) at ₹419 per share, subscribed 94.8x, listed on Jul 15, 2026.
  • Latest annual results (FY26): revenue ₹692 Cr (-11% YoY) and net profit ₹98 Cr (-12% YoY).
  • Ownership: promoter holding moved from 89.44% before the IPO to 74.72% after it.

3. Qualitative Analysis

a) Business Model

Core Products/Services

  • Woven, coated and laminated synthetic fabrics (polyamide and polyester with polyurethane)
  • Over 1,000 fabric types for aerospace, defence, industrial and outdoor uses
  • Finished defence products such as parachute systems and rapid deployment systems

Revenue Streams

Revenue comes from supplying technical fabrics to defence, aerospace, industrial and lifestyle customers and, increasingly, finished defence goods. Government orders and export demand drive volumes.

Competitive Advantages

  • High entry barriers and technical capability in engineered fabrics
  • Diversified end markets with long customer relationships
  • Experienced promoters supported by a professional team and a 2,077-strong workforce

b) Management Quality

Experience and track record: Incorporated in 1990, the company has a long technical track record and a three-year ROE of around 44%. Rising debtor days (from about 61 to 123) show that working capital discipline needs attention.

Corporate governance practices: The company is promoter-led. Our offer-document review found no auditor qualification (to be confirmed in the RHP). No promoter share pledge was disclosed in the offer document. Board independence, committee structure and related-party transactions should be read in the RHP before investing.

c) Growth Strategy

Expansion Plans

  • Expand into finished defence and aerospace products
  • Add capacity and widen the fabric portfolio

Research and Development Initiatives

No material standalone R&D programme is highlighted in the sources reviewed; competitiveness rests on manufacturing, sourcing and distribution execution.
